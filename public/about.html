new york compute club is a pooled gpu cluster that lives in new york. members rack their own hardware in apartments, offices, and one very good basement in red hook, and the club schedules jobs across all of it. you can bring a card, bring a workload, or bring both. both is our favorite.

the idea comes from an old crypto saying, not your keys, not your coins. if an exchange holds your coins, what you really hold is an iou from the exchange. open weights have ended up in the same place. you are licensed to run the model, but it doesn't fit on anything you own, so in practice you rent an 8x h100 node from one of a few clouds at whatever price they set that month. not your compute, not your model.

our answer is to own the building. every node in the club is member hardware inside the city limits. jobs travel between nodes as sealed boxes, so the scheduler only ever sees ciphertext, a node id, and a wattage number. the node that runs your job can read your job, which is why you pick your node.

the rule has a second version for later. not your power, not your model. the red hook basement has a generator and opinions on the subject.

the longer version of the argument is written down over at not your compute, not your model.

house rules

  1. nobody asks what you're running.
  2. heat is a feature. we will find a use for yours.
  3. no mining, please.
  4. nodes go down, it happens. a quick heads up in the group chat is all we ask.

the software

everything is built in the open and none of it is finished yet. come poke around.

  • nycc-engine · the inference engine every node runs.
  • nycc-grid · the mesh. scheduling, sealed jobs, and an untrusted coordinator.
  • newyorkcomputeclub · this website, manhattan included.

a good pull request moves your application up the pile. the grid itself is watchable at /grid.

applying

membership is small on purpose, and this box is the whole application. leave an email and we'll get back to you. we read everything, we are just slow about it. it helps if you've trained something, broken something, or own something with a fan.
